## 1. Reference-ad blueprint

- **Format** — 9:16 vertical, 360x640, deliberately low-fi. Photoreal *found UGC* — amateur phone clips of real people in real homes, intercut with stock 3D medical animation. Never a presenter, never a talking head. 100% voiceover-driven.
- **Hook device** — a **literal symptom shot with no face**: 0:00 opens on an extreme close-up of a man's bare calf mid-cramp, shot from his own eyeline, with the red caption `LOW MAGNESIUM` stamped over it. No intro, no greeting, no product. The problem is on screen before the first word lands.
- **The signature device — a draining meter.** A small skeuomorphic vertical gauge sits top-right from 0:00, labelled `100%` at the top and `Meter` at the bottom, coloured green→yellow→orange→red. It **drains one notch per symptom line** (full green at 0:02, down to a red sliver by 0:24). It carries the entire anaphora section and then disappears once the mechanism explanation starts. This is the single most copyable element in the ad.
- **Shot density** — 43 shots in 118.8s: **21.7 cuts/min, mean 2.76s, median 1.8s**. Relentless. Nothing is allowed to sit.
- **Framing / camera** — handheld, arm's length or over-the-shoulder, subjects usually looking *down at the painful body part*, not at the lens. Heavy motion blur on transitions, whip-pans, zoom-blurs. Bodies are cropped hard: a calf, a foot, two hands, a shoulder.
- **Recurring visual devices** — (a) the draining meter; (b) **white milk-splash wipes** between almost every clip, the dominant transition; (c) 3D anatomical inserts — red nerve networks, a blue digestive tract, floating blue `MAGNESIUM` molecule spheres, teal mineral crystal macro; (d) a grainy black-and-white "testimonial photo" treatment near the end.
- **Character and product roles** — **at least eight different people**, each appearing for one or two shots and never returning as a "presenter": a grey-haired man ~60 in a pink tee, a blonde woman ~55 in a recliner, an auburn-haired woman ~50 on a couch, a bearded man in glasses in a kitchen, a woman in a navy dress, a man in a grey polo, an older man in a red sweater, a woman in a floral robe. Nobody is the face of the ad — the *symptom* is. The **product appears only at 1:21 of 1:59** (a hand scooping white cream from a jar), i.e. the last 32% of runtime.
- **Caption treatment** — burned-in, bold condensed uppercase (Anton-style), thick black outline, centre-left lower third at ~62% frame height, max 2 lines of 3-4 words, building **word-by-word**. White is the base; **red marks the problem** (`LOW MAGNESIUM`, `TINGLING PAIN`, `WORSEN NERVE DAMAGE`) and **yellow marks the mechanism and the solution** (`THE NERVES THAT NEED IT`, `ISN'T ALWAYS THE SOLUTION`, `TRY`).
- **Narrative phases** — symptom anaphora under the draining meter ("low magnesium in your X shows up as Y", four times) → "most people never connect these" → mechanism (NMDA / overexcited receptors) → the pill is the wrong delivery route → topical bypasses digestion → product → daily-use proof → CTA. **The CTA is soft and verbal**, over a product shot; no button graphic, no countdown, no price.

**Pacing note for this build.** You asked for one clip per sentence (two where a sentence holds two scenarios), which gives 36 clips at a 3.62s mean — slower than the reference's 2.76s. To match the reference's density without re-writing the split, cut a hard punch-in *inside* the longer clips (11, 20, 21, 23, 25, 35) in the edit. Same generation, two shots.
